What is Mexican chili crisp?

It's salsa macha's cooler cousin: dried chiles, nuts, and seeds in a rich, crunchy oil. Spoonable heat you drizzle, dunk, and pile onto everything.

What does it taste like?

Toasty, nutty, a little smoky. Unexpected but familiar. You'll swear you've had it before, then realize you haven't.

Not your usual chili crisp.

Same crunch, different soul. Ours leans on Mexican chiles, nuts, and seeds: warmer and earthier, less salty-umami, more cocina. If Asian chili crisp is all about that savory, garlicky punch, ours is toastier and a little sweeter, built on the flavors of a Mexican kitchen.

Meet the lineup

Sweet

Toasty chiles with a warm, honeyed finish.

The one that surprises people. Mellow heat, a little sweetness, and that nutty crunch underneath. Hard to stop at one spoonful.

Smoky

Deep, roasty, a little moody.

The chiles do the talking here: low heat, big smoke, like something pulled straight off the comal. Made for eggs, beans, and anything grilled.

Spicy

The bold one.

Real heat, but still toasty and nutty underneath, so it's flavor first and fire second. For the people who reach for the hot sauce before they've even tasted the food.

Chili Oil

All the flavor of the crisp, none of the crunch.

Just a rich, chile-infused oil you drizzle at the end. A little goes everywhere: eggs, pizza, soup, a plate of avocado. Your hot honey, but make it Mexican.

What do I use it for?

Eggs, tacos, pizza, noodles, avocado toast. Anything that could use a little más. If it's food, it probably works.

    What is special about pirekua?

    Every great story begins with a dream — and ours began in Morelia, Michoacán, Mexico, with a group of friends who believed in the beauty of handcrafted Mexican products.

    After an earlier venture came to an end, Emma and Fernando weren’t ready to give up. When a family member introduced them to an unforgettable salsa macha, Fernando began experimenting with a recipe of his own.

    But every creative mind needs a dreamer who sees what an idea could become.

    Fernando perfected the recipe, while Emma saw its potential. With her determination, hard work, and belief in what they were building, she began making the salsa, sharing it with others, and turning their little idea into a growing business. Together, they transformed a recipe into a shared dream.

    They named it pirekua, meaning “song” in Purépecha, honoring their roots in Michoacán. What began with Facebook orders and the support of friends and family grew one jar at a time, through personal recommendations, hard work, and a community that believed in them.

    Along the way, Emma’s best friend, Elizabeth, became an essential source of encouragement, always supporting her growth and reminding her of the possibilities ahead.

    Even during the pandemic, when the world slowed down, pirekua found a way to bring people a little joy, delivering its salsa straight to their doors.

    Today, pirekua is more than salsa. It’s a story of partnership, friendship, resilience, and the people who believe in a dream before the world knows its name.

    Rooted in Michoacán. Made with love. Always meant to be shared.

    And the story continues…

    Every dream grows with the people who believe in it. And just when we think we know where the journey will take us, someone new comes along, sees the potential, and helps us imagine what comes next.

    pirekua has always been a story of people believing in something made with heart. And today, that story continues with new possibilities, new connections, and another reason to dream a little bigger.

    Here’s to the people who believe, the dreams that keep growing, and all the beautiful things still to come. Who knows what the next chapter will bring?
